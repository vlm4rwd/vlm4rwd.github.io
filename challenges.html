Challenge 01

VistaQA Zero-Shot Challenge

01

How well can a VLM answer correctly and ground its answers in the right visual evidence when evaluated zero-shot on VistaQA?

Participants may develop any vision-language model or system and evaluate it on VistaQA in a zero-shot setting. Training or fine-tuning on datasets other than VistaQA is allowed, and systems may use custom architectures, external tools, retrieval components, agents, or other inference-time methods. The challenge uses the official VistaQA evaluation protocol, with GROVE as the primary metric for jointly assessing answer correctness and visual grounding.

How to participate

  1. 1
    Register your team

    Complete the team registration form once per participating team by October 31, 2026.

  2. 2
    Build your system

    Use any model architecture, external training data, tools, agents, retrieval, or inference strategy, as long as VistaQA is not used for training, fine-tuning, or development.

  3. 3
    Generate predictions

    Produce answers and visual evidence predictions using the official format.

  4. 4
    Submit a system report through OpenReview

    Submit a system report describing your model or system, training data, external tools, agents, retrieval components, prompting strategy, and any other components used to produce the results. The report should also include links to your submitted evaluation results and, where available, the model, checkpoint, code repository, or system implementation.

Zero-shot requirement

VistaQA samples, labels, masks, or derived annotations may not be used for model training, fine-tuning, prompt optimization, model selection, hyperparameter tuning, or any other development step. Training and fine-tuning on external datasets are allowed. Participants may use custom model architectures, external tools, agents, retrieval systems, and other components, but must clearly disclose the complete system and all external resources used in their submission report. VistaQA is reserved for final evaluation only.

System Report Requirements

Each participating team must submit a system report through OpenReview by November 15, 2026. The report should be written in the workshop paper format and may be up to 4 pages, excluding references and appendix.

The report should clearly describe the complete system used to produce the submitted VistaQA results, including:

  • Team name and participating authors.
  • Model or system architecture and any training or fine-tuning performed.
  • External datasets and resources used.
  • Prompting strategies, external tools, agents, retrieval components, and other inference-time methods.
  • Final VistaQA results submitted for the leaderboard.
  • A link to the prediction and evaluation results used for verification.
  • Links to the model, checkpoint, code repository, or system implementation, where available.
  • Sufficient implementation and evaluation details to understand and reproduce the submitted approach.

Challenge 02

Grounding Failure Challenge

02

Show us where today’s VLMs fail to connect their responses to the right visual evidence.

We invite researchers to submit compelling real-world instances revealing grounding failures in vision-language models. We especially welcome cases that are surprising, systematic, practically important, or reveal risks relevant to real-world deployment.

What to submit

Media

An image, short video, screenshots, and one-page PDF.

Prompt

The prompt or question used to elicit the failure.

Models

Affected model family/families and exact versions where available.

Failure description

A concise explanation of what was grounded incorrectly and why the case matters.

What makes a strong submission?

01

Clear. The grounding failure is easy to understand and supported by the submitted evidence.

02

Insightful. The case reveals something meaningful about how current VLMs fail.

03

Relevant. The failure has implications for real-world use, reliability, or safety.

04

Reproducible. Stronger cases may recur across runs, prompts, or multiple models.

Recognition at VLM4RWD 2026

Selected and notable grounding failure cases will be highlighted and recognized during the workshop.
